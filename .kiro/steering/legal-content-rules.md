# Legal Content Rules — Mindful Mama

These rules apply to ALL content created for this project. They are non-negotiable.

## What We Are

We are a **psychoeducation and self-reflection tool** with practical life strategies. We are NOT a medical device, diagnostic tool, therapeutic service, or substitute for professional care.

## Language We NEVER Use

- "Based on your answers, you have ADHD"
- "Your score indicates clinical-level ADHD"
- "You should seek diagnosis based on these results"
- Any scoring that maps to DSM-5 criteria explicitly
- "Clinically validated" (in reference to our product)
- "Treatment plan" (in reference to what we provide)
- "This is CBT/DBT/therapy" (in reference to our content)
- "Proven to work" or any specific efficacy claims
- "Cured," "fixed," or "treated" in relation to outcomes
- Specific medication names or dosage recommendations
- "You have [any condition]" — we never diagnose
- "Your child has ADHD" or any child diagnosis language

## Language We CAN Use

- "Research shows..." (citing general findings)
- "Many people with ADHD report..."
- "You might find it helpful to..."
- "Some strategies that work for ADHD brains include..."
- "Consider discussing this with your healthcare provider"
- "This resonates with patterns common in ADHD"
- "For educational purposes" / "for self-reflection"
- "Informed by research on executive function"

## Testimonials

- NEVER fabricate testimonials presented as real
- If using illustrative examples, label them clearly: "Illustrative example"
- Once real testimonials exist, they must reflect genuine experiences
- Testimonials must NOT imply medical outcomes ("cured my ADHD," "no longer need therapy")
- Testimonials CAN describe emotional responses ("I finally felt understood") or behavioral changes ("The scripts helped me communicate better")

## Required Disclaimers

Every page must include (either inline or via global footer):

> "This product is for educational and self-reflection purposes only. It is not a medical device, diagnostic tool, or substitute for professional mental health care. No professional-client relationship is created through use of this product. If you are in crisis, contact 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or your local emergency services."

## Content Boundaries by Topic

### Medication
- ✓ CAN acknowledge medication exists and many find it helpful
- ✓ CAN say "discuss with your prescribing physician"
- ✗ CANNOT recommend specific medications
- ✗ CANNOT say "medication is safe during [pregnancy/breastfeeding]"
- ✗ CANNOT advise on timing, dosage, or adjustment

### Child Development
- ✓ CAN provide general developmental milestone information
- ✓ CAN say "some parents discuss these observations with their pediatrician"
- ✗ CANNOT imply we can identify ADHD in children
- ✗ CANNOT provide a child screening tool
- ✗ CANNOT say "your child has/might have ADHD"

### Hormonal Information
- ✓ CAN cite research on estrogen/dopamine relationship
- ✓ CAN say symptoms may fluctuate with cycle
- ✗ CANNOT advise on hormonal treatments
- ✗ CANNOT recommend supplements

### Mental Health (Burnout/Depression)
- ✓ CAN describe commonly reported differences between burnout and depression
- ✓ CAN strongly encourage professional support
- ✗ CANNOT diagnose depression or any mental health condition
- ✗ CANNOT say "you have burnout, not depression"

### Sleep
- ✓ CAN share general sleep hygiene strategies
- ✓ CAN note ADHD-sleep comorbidity research
- ✗ CANNOT diagnose sleep disorders
- ✗ CANNOT recommend sleep medications or supplements

## Therapeutic Techniques

We can use strategies INFORMED by therapy (cognitive reframes, coping cards, communication scripts) but we NEVER present ourselves as delivering therapy. The distinction:

- ✓ "Here's a helpful way to think about it"
- ✗ "This is your CBT intervention"

## Required Legal Pages (before accepting payment)

1. Terms of Service — no professional relationship formed, limitation of liability
2. Privacy Policy — data collection, storage, sharing, GDPR if serving EU
3. Refund Policy — clear terms

## The Golden Rule

When in doubt, ask: "Could a reasonable person interpret this as medical advice, diagnosis, or therapy?" If yes, reframe or add a disclaimer.
